CONTEXT

I'm evaluating a custom RTL PE (processing element) design for a paper submission
(ASP-DAC 2027). The PE is an N-way fused dot-product unit supporting Microscaling
(MX) formats: INT8xINT8, FPxFP (MXFP8/6), and FPxINT (mixed precision), at a
configurable fusion width N (N MACs fused per cycle via a pipelined tree with a
double-pumped accumulator, giving an initiation interval of 1 cycle despite a
4-stage pipeline). I used SCALE-Sim (a cycle-level systolic-array DNN accelerator
simulator) to get system-level throughput/utilization on real workloads, combined
with real PrimeTime PX synthesis results (TSMC 28nm, TT/0.8V/25C) for power and
area, to produce TOPS, TOPS/W, and TOPS/mm^2 figures.

I want a critical, skeptical review of the methodology and results below -- not
validation. Please point out anything that looks unsound, any claim that overreaches
what the evidence actually supports, any place a reviewer would push back, and
whether this is ready to go into a paper as-is.

METHODOLOGY (full detail in evaluation_methodology_paragraph.txt in this repo)

- SCALE-Sim has no native notion of operand precision or per-PE fusion width; it
  natively assumes 1 MAC/PE/cycle. So each workload is simulated once, unmodified,
  to get a baseline per-layer cycle count C_l and stall-cycle count S_l. The
  fused-N cycle count is then projected analytically:
      C_l(N) = S_l + ceil((C_l - S_l) / N)
  Stall cycles are left unchanged (memory traffic is independent of fusion width).
  This is NOT the same as shrinking the array size to fake fusion -- that approach
  was considered and rejected early on because it would also corrupt SCALE-Sim's
  memory-bandwidth accounting.
- Dataflow is output-stationary (OS), chosen because the PE's internally
  accumulating output register (partial sums held and updated locally per PE)
  matches OS semantics, not because it's the SCALE-Sim default (which is WS).
- Power/area are NOT modeled by SCALE-Sim at all; they're introduced entirely in
  post-processing from real per-PE synthesis figures, scaled by PE count:
      Power_array(N) = Power_PE(N) x PE_count
      Area_array(N)  = Area_PE(N) x PE_count / 1e6   [um^2 -> mm^2]
- Two array configurations were evaluated:
    (a) 32x32 (1024 PEs) at N=4, N=8, N=16 -- the primary evaluation.
    (b) 16x16 (256 PEs) at N=4 specifically, because 256 x 4 = 1024, matching
        32x32x1's total theoretical MAC/cycle capacity with fewer, wider PEs.
        This is a genuinely independent SCALE-Sim simulation at 16x16's own
        native fold pattern, not derived by rescaling the 32x32 numbers.
- Workloads: ResNet-18, AlexNet, ViT-S/B/L/H/bigG, Transformer-Fwd/InpGrad,
  MLPerf-Transformer (9-10 of these per configuration, see exclusions below).

KEY RESULTS

1. Realized fusion speedup vs. an analytical same-clock N=1 reference is exactly
   4.00x / 8.00x / 16.00x on every evaluated workload at the 32x32 array -- not
   approximately, exactly. This holds because every workload has zero stall
   cycles (fully compute-bound), so the cycle-projection formula above is exact.

2. 16x16 (256 PEs) x N=4 vs. 32x32 (1024 PEs) x N=1, using REAL synthesis for
   both (N=1: 0.56ns/6578.96um^2/3.7319mW per PE; N=4: 0.56ns/11302.58um^2/
   7.027mW per PE -- same clock period, so no frequency confound):
   - 2-18% FEWER cycles on all 9 tested workloads (not just matching, beating).
   - 2.17-2.51x better TOPS/W, 2.38-2.75x better TOPS/mm^2.
   - Per-PE, N=4 costs 1.72x the area and 1.88x the power of N=1 for 4x the peak
     MACs/cycle -- so the efficiency gain is that ratio compounded with the
     real-workload speedup, not an unexplained number.

3. Comparison against published MX designs (Cuyckens et al. ISLPED'25 and a
   follow-up NPU-integration paper, Dacapo/ISCA'24, Islamoglu MXdotp/ASAP'25) --
   competitive GOPS/W (full comparison table in evaluation_methodology_paragraph.txt),
   though this design is on TSMC 28nm vs. their 12-22nm nodes, and that gap is
   NOT normalized (raw numbers only, by explicit choice, not an oversight).

4. Fusion width N is uniform across INT8xINT8/FPxFP/FPxINT per-cycle (confirmed
   architectural property, not simulated) -- so throughput doesn't vary by
   precision combination, in contrast to competitor designs which reconfigure
   parallelism per mode (and show 6-9x energy swings between modes as a result).
   A preliminary (vectorless/case-analysis-estimated, NOT real switching-activity
   simulated) power breakdown suggests this design's power may also be much
   flatter across modes (~1.18x spread) than competitors, but this specific claim
   is explicitly not simulation-verified yet -- see
   PRELIMINARY_mode_power_estimate_NOT_FOR_CITATION.txt.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS / EXCLUSIONS (please check whether these are handled honestly,
not swept under the rug)

- LLaMA-3B-scale topology and a Transformer weight-gradient kernel were excluded
  from all array configurations: individual layers (thousands of channels at
  512x512, or a single 65536x8192x1024 GEMM) force SCALE-Sim to materialize
  per-cycle demand matrices exceeding available host RAM (36GB), even in
  isolation. This is stated as a scaling limitation of trace-based simulation,
  not a property of the PE.
- MLPerf-Transformer was excluded from the 16x16 comparison for runtime
  (891 layers, projected ~9-10 hours at 16x16's per-layer rate) and separately
  flagged as a utilization outlier in the 32x32 results (its topology decomposes
  attention into ~890 narrow per-head GEMMs, most of which occupy 1 row/column of
  a 32-wide array -- a benchmark artifact, not a PE deficiency, but real and
  reported, not hidden).
- The per-mode power breakdown (point 4 above) is vectorless/case-analysis
  estimated (PrimeTime set_case_analysis + set_switching_activity with standard
  0.5/0.5 random-data assumptions), not from real gate-level or RTL switching
  activity simulation. It has NOT been used anywhere in the main results tables
  or the comparison table -- only the single, real, per-N power figures have.
- No independent (outside this project) verification of any of these numbers has
  happened; I am the only party who has checked them, working alongside Claude
  in this repo.

WHAT I WANT FROM THIS REVIEW

1. Is the cycle-projection methodology (dividing compute-bound cycles by N,
   preserving stall cycles) defensible for a peer-reviewed venue, or does it need
   a caveat/justification I'm missing?
2. Is the 16x16/N=4 vs. 32x32/N=1 comparison a fair one, or is there a hidden
   asymmetry (e.g. in how SCALE-Sim schedules a narrower vs. wider array) that
   would make a reviewer skeptical of the "2-18% faster, not just equal" result?
3. Are there claims above that are stated more strongly than the evidence
   supports?
4. What would you ask for before trusting this enough to cite in your own work?
