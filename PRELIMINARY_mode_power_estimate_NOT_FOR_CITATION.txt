HOW THIS COMPARES -- the one claim that holds up even at this preliminary stage:

The distinguishing property visible here isn't the absolute GOPS/W (that number is
still a rough estimate, on a different node than every design it would be compared
against, and shouldn't be leaned on yet) -- it's how FLAT the efficiency is across
precision modes, which is a direct, checkable consequence of the design's confirmed
uniform-N-per-cycle behavior, not an artifact of this specific estimate:

    Design                    Max/min ratio across its own modes
    Cuyckens C1 (ISLPED'25)   8.74x   (2.43 pJ INT8 vs. 0.278 pJ FP4)
    Dacapo (ISCA'24)          7.49x   (2.68 pJ INT8 vs. 0.358 pJ FP4)
    Cuyckens C2 (2511.06313)  6.19x   (657 vs. 4065 GOPS/W)
    This work (preliminary)   1.18x   (7.548 vs. 6.414 mW)

Every published design surveyed here needs roughly an order of magnitude more energy
budget for its most expensive mode than its cheapest one, because they all reconfigure
parallelism per mode (1/4/8 or similar products-per-cycle depending on precision).
This design's fusion width N does not change with precision mode, so its power (and
therefore energy efficiency) stays within ~18% across INT8xINT8, FPxFP, and FPxINT --
a real architectural consequence of the uniform-N design choice, not a coincidence of
which toggle rates were asserted for this estimate. That claim is worth stating
plainly once real numbers back it: a system built on this PE doesn't need to
provision power delivery or thermal design for a worst-case mode that's 6-9x more
expensive than its best case, unlike every design compared against here -- it can be
designed close to one operating point regardless of which precision the workload uses.

This flatness claim is the one worth carrying into the actual paper once the numbers
behind it are real. The absolute GOPS/W numbers above are not ready for that yet.
